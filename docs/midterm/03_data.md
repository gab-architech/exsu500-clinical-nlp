# III. Data

**Source.** We use MTSamples, 4,999 sample medical transcription reports with about 40 specialty labels, scraped from mtsamples.com and distributed on Kaggle under a CC0 licence, with no credentialing required [17]. The data contain no patient identifiers. Model input is the `transcription` field only, because `keywords` contains the label in 97.9% of the rows that have keywords.

**Cleaning.** Table 1 lists each step. We removed 33 rows with no text and 938 rows whose label is a document type rather than a specialty (e.g. Discharge Summary). We also removed the 1,088 rows labelled Surgery, a procedure type: 1,039 are copies of notes already filed under a specialty, and the other 49 were set aside for review or exclusion. We then kept one example per unique normalized text (2,940 rows to 2,279 unique texts), so identical notes cannot appear in two piles. After mapping labels to 11 classes and excluding notes with no routing class, 2,230 notes remain.

**Classes.** Figure 1 shows the final distribution. Related labels were merged (e.g. Neurology with Neurosurgery), and General Medicine combines 8 raw labels, including Pain Management and Physical Medicine - Rehab. Class sizes range from 358 (Cardiovascular / Pulmonary) to 50 (Psychiatry / Psychology), a 7.2x imbalance.

**Label validation.** 606 notes needed a human decision: 578 filed under two or more specialties and 28 surgery-only notes. Two reviewers each read 303 notes in full, assigning 602 to a class and dropping 4. A keyword rule, not a trained model, offered a starting suggestion; it matched the final label in only 48.5% of cases. An AI assistant flagged 3 labels during a second audit, and the modelling lead confirmed the corrections.

**Blind audit.** One author (G.B.), blind to the existing labels, relabelled a stratified sample of 220 notes. On 60 human-reviewed notes, Cohen's kappa was 0.847 (95% bootstrap CI 0.742 to 0.941). On 160 auto-mapped notes, the error rate against the blind label was 13.8% (95% Wilson CI 9.3% to 19.9%). Of these 22 disagreements, 11 come from small labels that our class definition deliberately merges into General Medicine, including all 8 sampled Pain Management notes (7 placed under Orthopedic). We treat these as a definitional difference and report a sensitivity estimate without them: 6.9% (3.9% to 11.9%). No labels were changed.

**Split.** Before any modelling, we split the 2,230 notes 70/15/15 into training (1,560), validation (335) and test (335) sets, stratified by class with a fixed seed (42). The split is committed as note IDs only. The smallest class has 8 validation and 7 test notes, so its per-class results will carry wide confidence intervals.

**Limitations.** These are sample transcriptions from one source rather than documents from a real routing workflow, and the labels reflect how the site filed them, so results may not generalize. Class boundaries involve judgment: the blind audit found 6 of the 8 disagreements on human-reviewed notes involve Radiology (an imaging report can be filed as imaging or as the clinical specialty), and Pain Management notes filed under General Medicine mostly read as Orthopedic. The label string still appears in 8.7% of transcriptions; we will measure its effect with a masked-text variant. Deduplication handles identical texts only: a TF-IDF cosine check found 11 same-label note pairs at 0.8 or above across piles, touching 9 test and 4 validation notes. We keep the split and will report test macro-F1 with and without those test notes.
